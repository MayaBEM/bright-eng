The three sounds of −ED

Words ending in -ED aren't all pronounced the same way. The sound depends on the letter right before it. Tune in to the three sounds, listen to real examples, then test your ear in the practice round.

The rule

It depends on the final consonant sound

Say the word without -ED first. Whatever sound sits right at the end decides how -ED will be pronounced — not the spelling, the sound.

If the word already ends in a T or D sound, -ED adds a whole new syllable: /ɪd/. Everywhere else, it depends on whether your vocal cords vibrate for that final sound — if they don't, -ED sounds like a quick /t/; if they do, it sounds like a soft /d/.

Try the throat test

Touch your throat while you make the sound. A hum or vibration means it's voiced (→ /d/). No vibration means it's voiceless (→ /t/).
